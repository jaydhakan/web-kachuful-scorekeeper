## 1. Summary

The app is unusually well engineered: one colour source, lint against hex, contrast tests, reduced-motion handling, a 44px target test, real empty/error/skeleton states. The redesign problem is not missing craft. It is that the craft is spent on a look the brief rejects, and that only colour is tokenised.

### The 5 biggest problems

1. **The default theme is the template look.** Sunset Glass is an orange-to-pink gradient button over glowing peach, pink and violet blobs, with frosted glass on every surface (`src/styles/themes.css:84-130, 246-275`). On screen, every card, row, chip and tile is the same rounded glass pill. It is polished, but it has no point of view and is neither warm nor calm.
2. **Four themes, no identity.** Sunset Glass, Yerba Sun, Editorial Paper and Emerald Gilt share components but not a personality, so the product looks like a different app per device. None follows the system light/dark setting (`prefers-color-scheme` is used nowhere).
3. **Only colour is a token.** There are no spacing, shadow or duration tokens. The code has 47 distinct `shadow-[…]` values, about 15 radii, 26 type sizes (ten of them 1px apart), 20 line-heights, 20 letter-spacings, 28 animation durations and 10 spring configs.
4. **Everything shouts.** 84% of weight classes are bold or extrabold, and extrabold renders the same as bold in three themes because Instrument Sans stops at 700. The overshoot spring is used as often as the calm one (37 vs 38). The same fade-up entrance runs in 29 files. "Calm by default" is not possible on this base.
5. **The platform is half-built on `main`.** Every URL opens Kachuful (`src/App.tsx:21-24`); there is no landing page, no 404, and Kaali Teeri (about 8k lines) is mounted nowhere. Brand accent used as text fails WCAG AA on both light themes (2.5–3.2:1, 51 uses).

### The 5 biggest opportunities

1. **One identity, light and dark, built on the existing theme engine.** A new theme pair replaces the default and follows the system setting. The engine, lint rule and contrast test already exist, so this is mostly new values plus the missing tokens.
2. **The table is the brand.** The felt table with seats is the one image no other scorekeeper has. Make it the only saturated thing on screen and let everything else go quiet around it.
3. **A component diet.** One `Button` (variants and sizes), `ListRow`, `Switch`, `Badge` and `Input` replace about 110 hand-rolled instances, and remove the per-game duplicates before a third game copies them again.
4. **Motion with a budget.** The signature moments already exist and are good (deal fan, bid flight, turn light, ink-filled winner). Quieting everything else makes them land, and moving press/fade entrances to CSS wins back first-load JavaScript (currently over budget).
5. **A front door.** The landing page, per-game link previews and the 404 do not exist yet, so they can be designed in the new language once instead of being built in the old one and redone.

### How design tokens are defined

- **Colour:** CSS custom properties per `[data-theme]` in `src/styles/themes.css` (shared rules + Sunset Glass) and `src/styles/themes/<id>.css`. `src/theme/colors.ts` maps Tailwind colour names to those variables. ESLint rejects hex in components. This part is solid.
- **Materials:** about 25 classes (`.glass`, `.mat-fill`, `.mat-key`, `.mat-sheet`, `.felt` …) that components carry and each theme paints.
- **Type:** 26 font sizes in rem (`tailwind.config.ts:20-23`), three font roles per theme (`--font-display/body/mono`). No line-height or tracking tokens.
- **Radius:** `--r-sm/md/lg/xl` exist per theme but are used 5 times in tsx, against 124 `rounded-xl`/`rounded-2xl` and 18 arbitrary values.
- **Spacing, shadow, duration:** no tokens. Tailwind's default spacing scale; shadows written out at each use; `ENTER`/`EXIT` duration constants in `ui/motion.ts` have 0 uses.
- **Dark mode:** none as such. Two themes are dark, two are light; the admin picks a default and may let players choose. The system setting is never read.

## 4. Three design directions

All three keep the existing engine (theme files, material classes, lint, contrast test) and add what is missing: spacing, radius, shadow and motion tokens, and a light/dark pair that follows the system. Contrast ratios are computed (WCAG 2.x); "muted" is the weakest text allowed for anything a player must read.

### Recommendation: Direction A, Lamplight

- **It is the brief.** "Warm, cozy, polished, calm by default, impressive in specific places" describes A directly. B is warm but dry and has few places for a wow. C is warm but never calm; it is impressive everywhere, which is the problem the current default already has.
- **It fits how the app is used.** One phone lies on a table for two hours, often in the evening, glanced at by people who are mid-conversation. Low-chroma surfaces with one saturated object (the felt) read at a glance and do not tire. C's saturation would; B's fine rules and mono numerals are harder to read at arm's length.
- **It makes the product's one unique image the brand.** The table already exists in every game; A is the only direction built around it. B's hero is the score sheet, which every scorekeeper has.
- **It spans three games.** Kachuful, Kaali Teeri and Ishaara each already have an accent token (`--game-<id>`); on quiet paper those accents can carry each game. In C they would fight the palette.
- **It is the cheapest to keep accessible.** A's ratios clear AA on all three surfaces in both modes with room to spare. B's dark accent and C's light status colours each already need exceptions.
- **Its risk is blandness, and that is what the three signature moments are for.** B and C are worth keeping as ideas: B's tally marks could appear inside A's score book, and C's energy belongs in the streak and achievement moments.

Cost: one new font file (Fraunces, self-hosted like the others, so no npm dependency). Everything else is already in the repo.

## 11. Animation audit (deep pass)

Added 2026-11-13 at your request: every animation in the app, how good each one is, what to fix, where new motion would earn its place, and where the wow should come from. Still audit only; nothing was changed.

**How this was checked.** Every animated element was read in code and judged against a fixed rulebook (purpose and frequency, easing and duration, physicality, interruptibility, performance, accessibility, cohesion, missed opportunities). The headline claims were re-read at their cited lines before being written here.

**What this cannot tell you.** How motion _feels_ cannot be judged from code. Nothing was profiled, slowed down or watched on a phone. Items that depend on feel are marked _(feel-check)_. Kaali Teeri and the three non-default themes were read, not seen.

### 11.1 The short answer

- **The big moments are already there and mostly good.** The deal, the bid flying to its seat, the turn light, the staged score reveal, the ink-filled winner name, the podium, the streak burst: few scorekeepers have any of these.
- **They do not land as hard as they could, for two reasons.** Everything around them also moves (so nothing stands out), and each big moment spends its payoff in the first half-second instead of building to it.
- **The loudest thing in the app is its most frequent action.** One bid tap fires six effects, including a toast. A six-player round is about 36 animations and six toasts.
- **Browsing replays its entrances.** Every Back, and every leaderboard filter change, re-runs the list fade-up.
- **So the recipe for "more wow" is mostly subtraction plus timing.** Calm the frequent things, re-choreograph five existing moments, and add about eight new ones in places where something rare actually happened.
